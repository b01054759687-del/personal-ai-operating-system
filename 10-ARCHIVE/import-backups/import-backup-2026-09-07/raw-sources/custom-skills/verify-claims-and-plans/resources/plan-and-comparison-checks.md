## 1. Decision framing

Establish:

- Decision to make
- Decision-maker and intended user
- Desired outcome and baseline
- Must-Haves and Nice-to-Haves
- Budget, timeframe, geography, and constraints
- Risk tolerance
- Definition of success, failure, and “best”

If these materially affect the result and cannot be found, ask and stop.

## 2. Plan feasibility

Do not label a plan feasible until checking:

- Clear objective and measurable outcome
- Baseline and target
- Scope and exclusions
- Owner and decision authority
- Required skills and available capacity
- Budget and funding timing
- Timeline and realistic effort
- Dependencies and sequencing
- Technical and operational constraints
- Legal, regulatory, privacy, security, and safety requirements
- Stakeholders and adoption risk
- Measurement method and data availability
- Risks, mitigations, contingencies, and reversibility
- Success, failure, review, and stop criteria

Flag a plan as:

- `Feasible`
- `Feasible with Conditions`
- `Not Yet Decision-Ready`
- `Unrealistic under Current Constraints`
- `Insufficient Evidence`

## 3. Forecasts and scenarios

Separate:

- Historical Actual
- Current Actual
- Estimate
- Forecast
- Target
- Benchmark

When future results matter, provide:

- Best Case
- Base Case
- Worst Case

For every scenario, show:

- Assumptions
- Formula or model
- Input ranges
- Sensitivity drivers
- Dependencies
- Time horizon
- Confidence and limitations

Do not use a single-point forecast when the inputs are uncertain enough to
change the decision.

## 4. Fair comparisons

Compare the same:

- Date or period
- Geography and jurisdiction
- Currency and tax basis
- Product version
- Subscription plan
- Feature configuration
- User segment and use case
- Measurement definition
- Sample or evidence standard

Distinguish:

- Feature exists
- Feature is included in the selected plan
- Feature works in the user's region
- Feature quality is independently supported
- Feature is suitable for the specific workflow

Do not allow a vendor's missing information to become a favourable assumption.
Use `Insufficient Data`.

## 5. Scoring

- Define criteria before inspecting which option wins.
- Explain the measurement scale.
- Use weights only when they reflect the decision-maker's priorities.
- Ask for approval of material weights in high-impact decisions.
- Normalise incompatible units before aggregation.
- Do not score unsupported criteria.
- Show raw evidence next to scores.
- Run sensitivity analysis when small weight changes could reverse the winner.
- Do not hide disqualifying Must-Have failures inside a high total score.

## 6. Costs and alternatives

Calculate Total Cost of Ownership when relevant:

- Purchase or subscription
- Required add-ons or plugins
- Setup and integration
- Migration
- Training and change management
- Maintenance and support
- Operational labour
- Downtime and risk
- Exit and switching costs

Include:

- `Do Nothing / Keep Current Solution`
- Delay pending evidence
- Pilot or limited trial
- Alternative that better satisfies Must-Haves

## 7. Decision gates

Provide when relevant:

- `Go / No-Go Criteria`
- `Stop-Loss Criteria`
- Decision date
- Evidence owner
- Cheapest safe validation experiment
- Assumptions that must be tested first
- Trigger that would change the recommendation

A valid conclusion may be conditional, unresolved, or “none of the options”.
