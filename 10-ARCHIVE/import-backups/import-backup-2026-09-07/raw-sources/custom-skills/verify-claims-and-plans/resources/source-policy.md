## 1. Source selection principles

Select sources based on the claim, intended use, and consequence of error.
Prefer evidence closest to the original event, measurement, policy, product, or
decision.

Default hierarchy:

1. Primary authoritative sources
2. Transparent research, standards, regulators, and original datasets
3. Reputable specialist institutions and high-quality journalism
4. Identified expert analysis with disclosed methods and interests
5. User reports, communities, forums, and reviews
6. Vendor, affiliate, promotional, and unattributed content

Hierarchy is not automatic truth. Inspect methodology, limitations, and
applicability.

## 2. Source-quality dimensions

Assess each material source on:

- **Authority:** Is it responsible for or qualified to report this information?
- **Originality:** Is it the original evidence or a retelling?
- **Directness:** Does it directly support the claim?
- **Methodology:** Are collection, definitions, sample, and analysis explained?
- **Independence:** Is it independent of corroborating sources and interested
  parties?
- **Freshness:** Is it current enough for this claim?
- **Applicability:** Does it match the geography, population, version, plan,
  industry, and use case?
- **Transparency:** Are author, date, data, limitations, and corrections visible?
- **Conflict risk:** Is it sponsored, affiliate-funded, vendor-authored, or
  otherwise incentivised?
- **Consistency:** Does it conflict with stronger evidence?

Rate source quality as `Strong`, `Acceptable with limitations`, `Weak`, or
`Unsuitable for this claim`.

## 3. Domain routing

### Medical and health

Prefer current public-health authorities, regulators, recognised clinical
guidelines, systematic reviews, and peer-reviewed primary research. Check
population, outcome, dosage, study design, and publication status. Do not
diagnose or replace qualified clinical advice.

### Legal, regulatory, and policy

Prefer enacted legislation, official gazettes, regulators, courts, government
guidance, and authoritative legal databases. State jurisdiction, effective
date, legal status, and whether guidance is binding. Do not generalise across
jurisdictions.

### Financial, accounting, and investment

Prefer audited financial statements, regulatory filings, exchange or regulator
data, official rates, and transparent market datasets. Timestamp prices and
exchange rates. Separate historical fact, consensus estimate, scenario, and
advice.

### Software, platforms, and technical products

Prefer official documentation, release notes, changelogs, source repositories,
standards, security advisories, and reproducible tests. State version, plan,
region, environment, and deprecation status. Community posts may reveal real
issues but do not override current official behaviour without reproduction.

### Business, marketing, and operations

Prefer validated internal data for company performance, platform documentation
for platform mechanics, original market datasets, and transparent industry
research for benchmarks. Inspect attribution, sample, geography, company size,
industry, and measurement period. Treat vendor benchmarks as directional unless
independently validated.

### News and public events

Prefer original statements, documents, recordings, filings, or direct reporting.
Check the event date separately from publication date. Use independent reputable
outlets for contested or developing events. Mark developing information as
preliminary.

### Products, services, and purchasing

Prefer official specifications, current pricing, warranty and return policies,
independent testing, and verified owner experience. Separate feature
availability from feature quality. Include region, plan, tax, required add-ons,
and Total Cost of Ownership.

### People, jobs, and companies

Prefer official company pages, verified professional profiles, filings, job
postings, and direct public statements. Do not seek or expose private contact
details or sensitive personal information. Treat salary estimates and
third-party company profiles as estimates unless officially documented.

### Academic and scientific claims

Prefer peer-reviewed literature, recognised repositories, systematic reviews,
and original datasets. Check retractions, corrections, study design, sample,
effect size, uncertainty, and whether a preprint has passed peer review.

## 4. Independence and conflict checks

- Trace syndicated articles, press-release copies, and repeated statistics back
  to the origin.
- Count sources as independent only when their evidence or reporting is
  independently obtained.
- Identify funding, sponsorship, employment, affiliate links, and commercial
  incentives.
- Search for serious contradictory evidence and documented failure cases.
- Do not infer truth from popularity, search rank, repetition, or credentials
  alone.

## 5. Freshness and applicability

For mutable claims, record:

- Publication date
- Last-updated date
- Verification date
- Effective or event date
- Geography or jurisdiction
- Product and software version
- Plan or subscription tier
- Currency and tax basis
- Population, industry, or company size

Do not apply a benchmark unless the method and context are sufficiently
comparable.

## 6. Prohibited evidence shortcuts

Never treat these as final evidence:

- Search-result snippets
- AI overviews or generated summaries
- A citation title without opening the source
- An inaccessible citation whose content cannot be checked
- A homepage that does not contain the claim
- A fabricated or guessed URL
- An unattributed statistic
- A screenshot when the original is reasonably obtainable
- Several copies of the same underlying source as independent corroboration
- A review score without checking sample, date, and selection effects
- A vendor claim as neutral proof that its own product is best
